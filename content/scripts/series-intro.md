# Series Intro: Script, Storyboard and Filming Plan

(Master copy: the Claude doc at https://claude.ai/artifact/Y4n3ZJdhjNGqAs6Neg49tc. This file is a snapshot from Nov 23, 2026.)

A 35 to 40 second vertical video that sets up the series as a personal challenge: five free apps in five weeks, one every week until New Year. It doesn't say what the apps are, because each week's post does that. It needs no app footage at all.

Film it Fri Nov 27 with everything else, or any quiet moment before. Edit Mon Nov 30. Post Nov 30 if the edit is done, otherwise Dec 2.

## The idea in one line

"I'm challenging myself to vibe code five apps in five weeks for the holiday season, and you can use every one."

## Hook and payoff

- **Hook (first 5 seconds):** state the challenge in one sentence: you're challenging yourself to vibe code five apps in five weeks for the holiday season. Introduce yourself in the next shot, with the rules.
- **Stakes (middle):** the rules, and the honest part: some will go wrong, and you'll see that too. This is what makes it worth following and not just a product list.
- **Payoff (last 10 seconds):** what the viewer gets. Free tools to use or send to someone, a front-row seat to the whole challenge, and an open invitation to follow along and see whether you pull it off.
- **Call to action:** come along and see how it goes.

## What it needs (nothing built)

- You on camera, or just your voice (see the voice-only option below).
- Five week cards made in CapCut: palette background, "Week 1" to "Week 5", and a big question mark where the app would be. The mystery is part of the hook, and it means you never have to show anything that doesn't exist yet.
- One end card with your handle and the site address if it's live.
- Optional B-roll (slots below), each with a fallback that needs nothing built.

About 100 words, so roughly 35 to 40 seconds at an easy pace. Say it aloud once with a timer, and trim the rules line in shot 2 if it runs long.

## B-roll placeholders

Every slot works without footage. If you have something real by filming day, use it. If not, use the fallback. Never use a mock-up and call it a finished app.

| Slot | Where | If you have it | Fallback (needs nothing built) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Shot 2 | A 3-second screen recording of Claude Code working on something, any project | A 3-second clip of you at the laptop, or your hands typing. You can film this on Tuesday while building |
| B2 | Shot 3 | A fast flash of a real app screen between week cards, only once it exists | Nothing. The cards stand on their own |
| B3 | Shot 5 | The live site loading on your phone | Static end card. Use the live-site clip only if the address is really live |

Rule: if a slot is empty, the video should look finished without it.

## Hook options (record two or three, pick by how they feel to say)

- A (in the script): "I'm challenging myself to vibe code five apps in five weeks for the holiday season."
- B: "Five holiday apps, five weeks, all vibe coded by me. Let's see if I can do it."
- C: "I'm vibe coding five holiday apps in five weeks, and I don't know if I'll finish."

All three open by saying it's a challenge for yourself. C is the most playful, A is the clearest about what the viewer gets, and B is the most direct.

## Style notes (borrowed from Chris Raroque)

These come from his titles, descriptions and chapter lists. I haven't watched the videos or read transcripts, so treat them as structure to borrow, not as his voice to copy.

- **Plain self-intro, then straight to the point.** His descriptions open with who he is and what he builds. Yours: the challenge first, then "I'm Jason" and the rules in the next shot.
- **Honest hook.** His titles admit mistakes and reversals. Yours says you don't know if you'll pull it off.
- **Show the downsides and who it's for.** His videos have a downsides section and a "who should use it" section. Yours has "if you want free holiday tools, or you like watching someone build in public, come along" and "I'll show you either way".
- **Process over polish.** Say things the way you'd say them to a friend, in one or two takes. Don't read a script word for word. The script is a guide to the beats.
- **Candid titles for the weekly posts.** Examples of the pattern: "This simple feature almost broke my app", "I built X in 3 days, here's what went wrong", "The test that failed first".

## Caption and bio

Caption: "I'm challenging myself to vibe code 5 holiday apps in 5 weeks, one every week until New Year. No signup, nothing to install. Follow along and see if I pull it off."

Put the address in your bio before you post, and only if it is live. If it isn't, say "link coming soon" and drop the address from the end card.

## Filming order

1. Kit check: stand, wireless mic paired, window light in front of you, phone at eye level, Do Not Disturb on, 10 GB free. Do a 10-second test.
2. Shot 4 first (it's the payoff, so get the energy right), three takes, with a clap at the start of each.
3. Shot 2 and shot 3's voiceover, three takes each.
4. Shot 1 last, two or three hook options, three takes each. You say it best once you know what follows.
5. Ten seconds of room tone.
6. Optional: any B-roll that exists, filmed in the same session.

## Voice-only option

If being on camera feels like too much that day, record the voiceover alone and run it over the week cards and any B-roll. The video still works, and it counts as posted. You can do a face version for a later episode.

## Edit notes (CapCut, Mon Nov 30)

Cut every 2 to 3 seconds, burn the captions in (most people watch muted), and put a small "1/5" tag in the corner of each week card so it reads as a series. Check the finished video is under 40 seconds and makes sense with the sound off.

## Done when

The video is under 40 seconds, it states the challenge in the first 5 seconds, the viewer knows what they get and that they're invited to follow along, it reads fine on mute, and nothing on screen claims an app exists that doesn't.

## Still open

Which platforms you will post to. It changes the end card and caption style, not the video.
